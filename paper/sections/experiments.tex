In this section, we present benchmark results obtained using our unified benchmarking interface described in Section~\ref{sec:evaluation}. We report results on real-world graphs with and without ground-truth labels, as well as synthetically generated graphs with controlled noise in structure and features. Rather than exhaustively ranking algorithms, these experiments aim to illustrate how different algorithmic design choices behave under a standardized benchmarking setup.

\subsection{State-of-the-Art Comparisons}

Figure~\ref{fig:comparisons} shows the main results of our experimental comparison. Across both datasets, \textsc{VieClus} achieves the highest modularity whenever it completes within the imposed resource limits, consistent with prior findings~\cite{vieclus2018,maxsat2025}. As expected for a memetic search-based method, it exceeds the memory limit on the largest DIMACS instances.

On the \textsc{Ground-Truth} dataset, many methods achieve similar high F1 scores. Among them, \textsc{MAGI} achieves the strongest and most stable performance. This highlights a general mismatch between modularity optimization and externally defined communities. For example, the Citeseer ground-truth clustering has a modularity of 0.54, whereas all methods produce clusterings with modularity above 0.54. This discrepancy reflects the fact that citation behavior does not necessarily align with categorical labels, as Figure~\ref{fig:comparisons} also indicates.

For the \textsc{DIMACS} graphs, differences between methods are more pronounced, with modularity-focused algorithms clearly outperforming learning-based approaches. Among learning-based methods, performance varies substantially depending on whether the method is designed to operate without node features, with \textsc{GNNS} performing best in this setting.

The streaming methods \textsc{Hollocou} and \textsc{CluStRE} trade solution quality for extreme efficiency. While their F1 scores are lower on the \textsc{Ground-Truth} dataset, \textsc{CluStRE} achieves competitive modularity on DIMACS instances while using orders of magnitude less time and memory. The exact algorithm \textsc{Bayan} terminates only on the eight smallest instances from the \textsc{DIMACS} dataset and is therefore excluded from the performance profiles. For these graphs, \textsc{VieClus} found the optimal clustering for all of them, \textsc{Leiden} for 7/8, \textsc{GNNS} for 5/8, \textsc{COMBO} for 4/8, and \textsc{Louvain} for 3/8. The remaining algorithms did not find any known optimal solutions.

\subsection{Quality-Efficiency Tradeoffs}

Figure~\ref{fig:efficiency} summarizes the tradeoff between clustering quality and execution time using geometric means across instances. Streaming methods occupy the extreme low-latency regime, while greedy modularity methods (\textsc{Louvain}, \textsc{Leiden}) achieve a favorable balance between quality and efficiency. The search-based algorithm \textsc{VieClus} achieves the highest quality, but at a substantially higher computational cost. Similarly, learning-based methods generally attain high solution qualities at substantially higher computational cost.

Peak memory usage varies by several orders of magnitude across methods; we provide detailed measurements for the \textsc{Ground-Truth} dataset in Appendix~\ref{sec:memory}. We also evaluate the parallel algorithms sequentially to show how additional compute resources affect these methods; we provide detailed measurements in Appendix~\ref{sec:par-seq}.

\subsection{Syntetic Graphs}

\subfile{../figures/adcsbm_plot.tex}

To further evaluate the impact and sensitivity of graph features across clustering algorithms, we include experiments with syntactically generated graphs with vertex features. These experiments illustrate how feature-aware methods respond to controlled degradation of structural and attribute signals, rather than ranking the methods. For reference, we also include \textsc{VieClus}, which performed best among the pure modularity-optimizing methods. We conduct two sets of experiments with the ADC-SBM generator. First, we generate a sequence of graphs with gradually higher $d_{out}$, causing the graph structure to carry less information about the original $k$ clusters. And second, we generate graphs with strong community structure as we gradually increase the cluster center variance $\sigma_c$, leading the graph features to carry less information about the clusters.

Figure~\ref{fig:adcsbm} summarizes the results of the two ADC-SBM experiments. When the feature signal is strong, \textsc{MAGI} and \textsc{DMoN} achieve the highest NMI scores, and beyond $d_{out} \approx 11$, all feature-aware methods outperform \textsc{VieClus}. The results also highlight clear differences in how methods balance structural and feature information. \textsc{MAGI} relies primarily on node features, as evidenced by its strong performance under structural noise and its sensitivity to feature degradation. In contrast, \textsc{DMoN} remains robust in both settings, indicating a more balanced use of topology and attributes. \textsc{CommDGI} exhibits similar trends but performs consistently worse than \textsc{DMoN}. \textsc{DGCluster} shows the opposite bias to \textsc{MAGI}, favoring graph structure over features, while \textsc{UCoDe} displays a similar but less pronounced tendency. We exclude \textsc{LIM} from this figure, as it consistently collapsed to a single cluster, yielding zero NMI across all configurations.
